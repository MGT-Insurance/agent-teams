Agent memory landscape research plan

A primary-source research program to decide whether agent-teams should keep, incrementally improve, replace, or combine parts of its current memory system while preserving the behaviors that already work.

Summary

The current custom memory system is useful but spans storage, synchronization, command behavior, prompts, hooks, curation instructions, runtime-specific startup paths, and dashboard surfaces. The research will compare that system against external platforms, framework-native libraries, composable components, and hybrid architectures using one required-capability contract and one evidence ledger. The unchanged system and a bounded incremental-improvement option will be evaluated independently, not used as an undifferentiated baseline. Work proceeds from contract approval to parallel evidence collection, then joins for live validation, reproducible scoring, independent review, and a final recommendation. The research does not implement or migrate anything.

Questions requiring human judgment

Decision 1 · optimization target

After R1–R7 pass, should maintenance reduction be the dominant decision factor?

Recommended default: yes. Treat portability, export, rollback, and privacy as safeguards that cannot be traded away for a lower maintenance estimate.

Decision 2 · hosted-service appetite

May a hosted service win if it materially reduces maintenance?

Recommended default: keep hosted and self-hosted editions as separate candidates; allow a hosted winner only when data handling, export fidelity, recurring cost, outage behavior, and rollback are explicit and acceptable.

Decision 3 · useful-memory signal

What quality bar should R7 require?

Recommended default: automatic application events with provenance and outcome context are a pass; voluntary agent self-reporting alone is partial because it is expected to undercount and can be lost under concurrency.

Decision 4 · curation authority

How much autonomous deletion or conflict resolution is acceptable?

Recommended default: automate reversible promotion, demotion, duplicate detection, and suggested merges; require a reviewable record for semantic conflict resolution and destructive eviction until validation shows a lower-risk policy.

Problem and desired outcomes

Repeated instances of the same role need to accumulate durable operational knowledge across sessions and machines. The best knowledge must arrive automatically in a small context budget; the long tail must remain searchable; and curation must keep the system from growing without bound. These behaviors currently work well enough to be valuable, but maintaining them requires coordination across multiple custom layers.

Desired outcome

  • A defensible keep, improve, buy, or hybrid recommendation.
  • Lower owned maintenance without losing R1–R7.
  • A clear boundary between vendor/library responsibility and agent-teams responsibility.
  • A reversible adoption path with explicit retained custom code.
  • Evidence that another reviewer can reopen and reproduce.

Decision quality

  • No winner is assumed by this plan.
  • Roadmap and marketing claims cannot carry a recommendation.
  • Hosted and self-hosted capability surfaces remain separate.
  • Unknown means unknown, not zero and not absent.
  • Maintenance moved into adapters still counts as maintenance.

Current system at a high level

Observed architecture

  • Storage and sync: role memories live in the global Dolt-backed Beads workspace and synchronize through refs/dolt/data.
  • Tiering: keys encode fresh, hot, and cold tiers. Fresh and hot are returned by ateam learnings; cold is available through ateam recall.
  • Writes: ateam learn is an upsert with per-entry byte caps. ateam forget removes a tier-specific key with recovery available from Dolt history.
  • Curation: ateam condense-check gates work based on fresh material. A prompt-driven condense process designs and applies a hot set, then drains fresh to cold under an advisory lock.
  • Use tracking: ateam applied stores a tier-independent count and timestamp through a non-atomic read-modify-write operation.
  • Delivery: Claude Code uses hooks plus role self-fetch instructions; Codex uses plugin hooks, skills, and generated custom-agent definitions. The dashboard reads a normalized ateam memories-json view.

Maintenance concerns to measure, not presume

Distributed contract

Behavior is coordinated across Go verbs, role prompts, hook scripts, skills, generated agent definitions, dashboard types, tests, and prebuilt binaries.

Runtime delivery

Claude Code and Codex have different startup and subagent mechanisms, limits, and recovery paths. Cross-harness parity requires explicit upkeep.

Curation operation

The current workflow combines deterministic commands with model-driven judgment and ordering-sensitive instructions. Its safety depends on lock, drain, and recovery conventions.

Retrieval quality

Cold retrieval is custom tokenized substring ranking. Relevance, scale, and false-positive behavior need an empirical baseline.

Telemetry fidelity

Applied counts are self-reported and non-atomic. The research must measure what that signal proves and where it can undercount.

Operational ownership

Dolt synchronization, release/version parity, hook reliability, token budgets, prompt contracts, and dashboard compatibility remain project-owned today.

Primary repository surfaces for the baseline: README.md; plugins/agent-teams/CLAUDE.md; internal/verbs/query.go, write.go, kong_converted.go, and condense_check.go; plugins/agent-teams/hooks/**; plugins/agent-teams/roles/**; plugins/agent-teams/skills/condense/**; plugins/agent-teams-codex/**; internal/verbs/codex_agents/**; and the dashboard memory API, types, and view.

Primary-source evidence and scoring

Evidence grades

GradeMeaningPermitted use
A · reproducedBehavior reproduced from a pinned release, commit, executable, or disposable probe and paired with current official documentation or repository source.May support required-capability gates and load-bearing recommendation claims.
B · primary documentedExplicit current official docs, API/SDK reference, repository source, release notes, or primary paper, pinned by version or commit and access date, but not reproduced locally.May support a gate when the behavior cannot reasonably be run and the limitation is explicit.
C · directionalMaintainer issue, discussion, roadmap, or primary paper whose applicability to the candidate is not demonstrated.May identify a lead or unknown; cannot independently pass a required gate.
D · discovery onlyVendor marketing, secondary analysis, community commentary, or inference.May identify what to verify; cannot support a score or recommendation.

Every scored claim records candidate and edition, version or commit, source URL or repository path, source type, access date, a paraphrased claim, evidence grade, reproduction command or artifact when applicable, and uncertainty. Newer observed behavior outranks conflicting prose. Inaccessible or unsupported behavior remains unknown.

Required-capability gate

  • Each candidate receives pass, partial, fail, or unknown for each of R1–R7.
  • A standalone recommendation must pass every required capability with grade A or B evidence.
  • Partial, fail, or unresolved unknown blocks a standalone recommendation. The candidate may remain as a hybrid only when the missing adapter, owner, maintenance cost, and validation plan are explicit.
  • Hosted and self-hosted editions are gated independently. Provider-native behavior is not cross-harness unless a neutral exportable boundary is proven.

Non-gating score

Only candidates that clear the gate, or explicit hybrid architectures that fill each gap, receive a weighted score. Each dimension uses a documented 0–4 scale: 0 unacceptable or absent, 1 major gaps, 2 workable with material burden, 3 strong with bounded caveats, 4 strong and directly evidenced. Unknown is not converted to zero; it remains unscored and blocks ranking until resolved or accepted as a human risk.

DimensionWeightWhat is scored
Expected maintenance reduction20%Owned components retired, update burden, prompt/hook/runtime coordination removed, and maintenance merely shifted into adapters.
Integration and retained custom code15%Adapter count, code surface, runtime dependencies, and change isolation.
Harness portability and coupling15%Neutral CLI/API/protocol support and independence from one orchestrator or model provider.
Data ownership, export, migration, rollback12%Schema transparency, complete export, backup/restore, dual run, and reversibility.
Security and privacy10%Data flow, tenancy, retention, encryption, credential scope, and local/self-host controls.
Maturity and reliability10%Release health, compatibility discipline, failure behavior, concurrency, and recovery.
Operational burden8%Services, upgrades, monitoring, storage operations, incident response, and offline behavior.
Cost6%License, service, model, embedding, storage, and engineering costs under stated usage assumptions.
Licensing and project risk4%License constraints, governance, lock-in, maintenance activity, and abandonment risk.

Sensitivity check: recompute with equal weights, then vary the three largest weights by ±25% while keeping totals normalized. A winner that changes under plausible weights is reported as decision-sensitive, not definitive. Raw scores, weighted scores, formulas, exclusions, and evidence rows remain visible together.

Proposed execution sequence

0 · Human approvalFreeze requirements, candidate families, rubric, questions, and authorization boundary.
1 · Smallest closed research loopBaseline both internal candidates; broad-screen every family; select one evidenced representative per viable family.
2 · Deep evidenceRun file-disjoint family and quality tracks in parallel; expand only candidates that can retire a real maintenance layer.
3 · Validation joinExercise one shared scenario set against the internal candidates, strongest external option, and strongest hybrid.
4 · Decide and auditScore, sensitivity-test, independently review, resolve findings, and author the final recommendation.

This sequence is concentric. Phase 1 closes a small end-to-end research loop before deep profiles expand: contract → current baseline → broad family coverage → shortlist rationale. Deep research does not begin for candidates that cannot plausibly own a high-maintenance layer or cannot provide primary evidence. Validation is a join, not a separate parallel opinion, and the final report remains blocked on independent evidence review.

Deliverables and explicit non-goals

Deliverables after approval

  • Five source-owned evidence artifacts under docs/research/agent-memory/.
  • A validation and migration artifact with reproducible scenarios and risk register.
  • A transparent decision matrix with raw evidence, gates, weights, and sensitivity results.
  • An independent evidence review with finding dispositions.
  • A final report at docs/research/agent-memory-landscape.md recommending keep, improve, buy, or hybrid.
  • A staged migration and rollback outline if the recommendation changes the system.

Non-goals

  • No feature implementation, refactor, migration, deployment, or production write.
  • No product winner or architectural conclusion in individual research tracks.
  • No exhaustive benchmark of every vector database or agent framework.
  • No acceptance of roadmap, marketing, or secondary claims as decision evidence.
  • No silent expansion into project knowledge, user-preference, or machine-local instruction redesign beyond interfaces that affect R1–R7.
  • No reusable probe code checked into the repository without separate authorization.
